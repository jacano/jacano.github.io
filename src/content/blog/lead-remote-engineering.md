As Head of Engineering at Dedge Security I lead a distributed team. The team builds a critical Web3 platform. This is my playbook.

---

## Rituals that add value

- Do weekly demos, not endless status updates.
- Ask the team to draw architecture diagrams. A diagram is the fastest way for another person to understand a system.
- Keep the diagrams in the repository and update them with the code.
- Provide on-call with blameless postmortems.

---

## Give oxygen to the team

This is the lesson that helped me the most. An experienced engineering manager told me: give oxygen to the others, always inside the team.

As a manager you see the good problems first. You know the hard bug, the new tool, the design that nobody solved yet. The easy move is to keep them. Do not do it.

- The investigations that motivate an expert and challenge them must go to that expert. Not to you.
- A good engineering task is the best way to grow a person and to keep a person.
- If you come from a technical role, your hands want the work. This is the trap. Take a step back.
- Your job is to remove the blockers and to give the context. The team does the work.
- Give a person the space to solve a problem their own way. The result can be better than yours.

---

## Metrics that matter

Measure delivery. Do not turn one number into a game. Ask this question: how long does a critical fix need to reach production? Look at the trend, not at one number. The team must feel safe to report a bad number.
